# Final-Year Project Viva: Examiner Q&A Guide

This guide contains realistic questions typically posed by external examiners, university evaluators, and industry technical panels during final-year engineering project defenses, paired with concise, technically rigorous, and honest answers.

---

### Q1: Why do you need a blockchain at all? Couldn't you achieve the same result using a traditional PostgreSQL database with digital signatures?
**Answer**:
A traditional database, even with digital signatures, suffers from a **single point of trust and centralized administrative vulnerability**:
1. A database administrator (DBA), hosting provider, or compromised server can rewrite rows, alter timestamps, or delete historical records without leaving a verifiable external audit trail.
2. If the hosting organization shuts down or suffers ransomware, verification ceases globally.
3. In contrast, Ethereum provides **immutable, multi-party consensus**. Once an accredited university commits a credential hash or `marksHash`, no one—not even the platform administrator—can alter or delete that record. It establishes decentralized, non-repudiable truth.

---

### Q2: Why did you choose Ethereum/EVM rather than a private consortium blockchain like Hyperledger Fabric?
**Answer**:
1. **Public Verifiability**: Any third-party recruiter, multinational employer, or overseas university can verify a candidate's credentials using standard JSON-RPC without needing permission to join a private consortium network.
2. **Ecosystem Standardization**: Ethereum standards (ERC-20, EIP-712, EIP-4361 SIWE) and developer tooling (Ethers.js, Hardhat) are the global industry standard.
3. In production, this architecture can be deployed to an Ethereum Layer-2 rollup (Base, Arbitrum, Optimism) to achieve the sub-second finality and near-zero fees of a consortium chain while retaining the decentralization of Ethereum Layer-1.

---

### Q3: Who pays the gas fees when transactions are written to the blockchain?
**Answer**:
The platform utilizes an **Authorized Relayer Pattern**. 
End users (university staff, recruiters, and students) pay **zero gas fees** and do not need to hold native cryptocurrency. Instead, authenticated API calls are signed and submitted by a designated backend relayer wallet (`authorizedRelayer`) funded by the platform authority. The smart contract validates that write calls originate from this authorized relayer.

---

### Q4: If blockchain is immutable, how do you comply with GDPR Article 17 ("Right to be Forgotten")?
**Answer**:
By design, **zero Personally Identifiable Information (PII) or plaintext marks are stored on-chain**:
- The blockchain stores only a 32-byte pseudo-random hash (`marksHash`) and the student's USN.
- All personal data (email, telephone, subject breakdown) resides in the off-chain PostgreSQL database.
- If a candidate requests data erasure, their database profile is purged. The on-chain `marksHash` remains as a mathematically irreversible string (pre-image resistance) from which no personal data can ever be reconstructed.

---

### Q5: Can College A register students or issue credentials on behalf of College B?
**Answer**:
**No. Cross-college operations are strictly blocked by three independent layers of defense**:
1. **USN Syntax Validation**: Every USN must match the standardized nomenclature `^\d[A-Z]{2}\d{2}[A-Z]{2}\d{3}$`.
2. **Session-Level College Binding**: The 2-letter college code extracted from the USN (e.g. `RV` from `1RV21CS001`) must match the authenticated college code stored in the caller's verified JWT session (`session.entityInfo.code`).
3. **Database & Queue Isolation**: Colleges can only query, review achievements for, or issue credentials to USNs belonging to their own college code.

---

### Q6: What happens if a rogue developer with direct PostgreSQL access alters a student's marks in the database?
**Answer**:
The tampering is **detected immediately upon verification**:
- When the college originally registered the student, a Keccak-256 hash of their marks was permanently committed to the smart contract (`marksHash`).
- If an attacker manually modifies marks in the `StudentMark` or `StudentProfile` database tables, the re-computed hash will no longer match the on-chain `marksHash`.
- The verification engine flags the discrepancy as an unauthorized database mutation.

---

### Q7: If all write operations pass through a backend relayer, doesn't that make the system centralized?
**Answer**:
**It is a pragmatic hybrid architecture**:
- The relayer provides a smooth, gasless user experience (Web2 UX with Web3 security).
- **The relayer cannot forge credentials**: It can only submit transactions that satisfy the business rules and cryptographic signatures generated by accredited university sessions.
- In our future roadmap, we plan to transition to **ERC-4337 Account Abstraction**, where universities sign EIP-712 typed data payloads and decentralized paymasters sponsor gas, eliminating relayer centralization entirely.

---

### Q8: What happens if the relayer wallet's private key is leaked or compromised?
**Answer**:
1. **Contract Isolation**: The smart contract includes an administrative key rotation function (`setAuthorizedRelayer`) callable only by the contract deployer to instantly revoke a compromised relayer and authorize a new key.
2. **Data Constraints**: Even with a leaked relayer key, an attacker cannot forge an accredited university without also passing on-chain checks (`universityId` must be active and match the registered college code).

### Q9: Why did you use IPFS (Pinata) instead of conventional cloud storage like AWS S3?
**Answer**:
1. **Content Addressing vs. Location Addressing**: In AWS S3, files are located by URL (`s3.amazon.com/bucket/file.pdf`). An S3 administrator can replace the file at that URL at any time.
2. In IPFS, a file's address is its cryptographic content hash (CID). If an attacker changes a single bit of the PDF, the CID changes completely.
3. Anchoring the IPFS CID to the smart contract guarantees that the document retrieved 10 years from now is bit-for-bit identical to the document issued today.

### Q10: If IPFS is a public decentralized network, can anyone see private student marksheets?
**Answer**:
No. VeriChain implements **Client-Side AES-256-GCM Encryption**:
- Universities can encrypt the marksheet PDF in the browser using AES-256-GCM before uploading it to Pinata IPFS.
- The ciphertext pinned to IPFS is undecryptable without the symmetric key.
- Only the candidate and authorized recruiters with the secret decryption key can decrypt the document in their browser.

---

### Q11: How do you prevent brute-force guessing of institutional access passkeys and student OTPs?
**Answer**:
1. **Bcrypt with Dummy Compare**: Institutional codes are checked using constant-time dummy comparisons to defeat side-channel timing analysis.
2. **Distributed Rate Limiting**: The system tracks failed attempts in PostgreSQL (`RateLimitCounter`) across IP and identifier.
3. **Automatic Lockout**: 5 consecutive failed attempts trigger an immediate **5-minute (300-second) hard lockout** returning HTTP 429.
4. **OTP Invalidation**: Student OTPs are permanently invalidated after 5 failed attempts and expire automatically after 10 minutes.

---

### Q12: Why don't students have passwords to access their digital lockers?
**Answer**:
Passwords are the leading cause of credential breaches, phishing, and account takeovers.
VeriChain uses **passwordless multi-factor authentication**:
- **Factor 1**: The student enters their institutional USN.
- **Factor 2**: A single-use 6-digit OTP (HMAC-SHA256 hashed in DB) is delivered to their verified university email on file.
- **Factor 3 (Optional)**: Sign-In with Ethereum (SIWE / EIP-4361) using their linked MetaMask wallet.

### Q13: What is the difference between Mode 1 and Mode 2 verification in the corporate recruiter portal?
**Answer**:
- **Mode 1 (Quick Screening)**: Takes a candidate's USN and queries the blockchain and database to verify enrollment, college accreditation, degree course, and active credential status in $\le 1$ second.
- **Mode 2 (Deep Cryptographic Audit)**: Takes an uploaded PDF document, calculates its SHA-256 hash client-side in the browser, and performs a bit-for-bit match against the on-chain `documentHash`. It detects if even a comma, date, or decimal point was altered.

---

### Q14: Why do you calculate the document SHA-256 hash in the recruiter's browser rather than on the server?
**Answer**:
**Zero-Knowledge Verifier Trust**:
If the server computed the hash, the recruiter would have to trust that the server didn't alter the file during transmission. By calculating the hash in the browser using the W3C Web Crypto API (`crypto.subtle.digest`), the recruiter inspects the file on their own machine, guaranteeing that the document in their hand matches the blockchain anchor without placing blind trust in any intermediate web server.

### Q15: Can one student link multiple MetaMask wallets, or can one wallet claim multiple students?
**Answer**:
**Strict 1-to-1 linkage is enforced at the database level**:
In [`prisma/schema.prisma`](file:///c:/Users/dhany/Desktop/Project/prisma/schema.prisma), the `StudentWalletLink` model enforces:
```prisma
model StudentWalletLink {
  usn    String @unique
  wallet String @unique
}
```
This guarantees that 1 student USN can link to at most 1 wallet, and 1 Ethereum wallet can never claim more than 1 student identity.

### Q16: How does the system handle marks revaluation, correction, or semester updates?
**Answer**:
Through **Audited Versioning**:
- The database maintains a `StudentMarkVersion` table tracking historical versions (`version: 1, 2, ...`).
- When marks are updated, the previous state is archived, and a new `marksHash` is generated and anchored on-chain.
- The `StudentProfile` updates its `marksVersion` counter and records the transaction hash of the update, ensuring full transparency regarding who made the change and when.

### Q17: What are the primary technical limitations of your PDF marksheet parsing engine?
**Answer**:
**Honest Answer**:
Our current PDF parsing uses regular expression extraction over embedded digital text streams (`pdf-parse`). Its limitations are:
1. It relies on digitally exported PDFs; scanned paper documents (raster images) require an OCR engine like AWS Textract or Tesseract.
2. Variations in table formatting across different autonomous colleges can occasionally misalign subject columns.
For these reasons, we provide a standardized **Excel (`.xlsx`) and CSV upload template** that guarantees 100% deterministic parsing.

### Q18: If 50,000 students graduate at once, can this system handle the load?
**Answer**:
1. **Web & Database Tier**: Highly scalable. Next.js serverless functions scale automatically, and Neon PostgreSQL uses serverless connection pooling.
2. **Blockchain Tier**: Ethereum mainnet or Sepolia processes $\approx 15\text{ tx/sec}$. Submitting 50,000 individual transactions directly would take nearly an hour.
3. **Production Solution**: Implement **batch Merkle root anchoring**. Rather than registering 50,000 students in individual transactions, the university combines all 50,000 `marksHash` values into a single **Merkle Tree**, commits the single 32-byte Merkle Root on-chain in 1 transaction, and issues cryptographic Merkle proofs to individual students.

### Q19: If this project were taken to production next week, what would you upgrade first?
**Answer**:
1. **Layer-2 Rollup Migration**: Deploy from Sepolia to an Ethereum Layer-2 (Base, Arbitrum, or Polygon) for sub-second confirmations and sub-penny gas costs.
2. **Account Abstraction (ERC-4337)**: Replace the single relayer key with smart contract wallets and paymasters.
3. **Decentralized Relayer Networks**: Integrate Gelato or OpenZeppelin Defender to eliminate single-server relayer bottlenecks.
